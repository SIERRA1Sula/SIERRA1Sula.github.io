01 · FINAL YEAR PROJECT

Domestic Food Waste Dryer with Grinding & Pelletizing

Development of an integrated household-scale system to process domestic food waste into biomass fuel pellets.

Download Project Report ↓ All Projects

OVERVIEW

Project Overview

The project investigates a domestic-scale process that combines moisture reduction, size reduction, mixing and pelletizing. The intended outcome is to convert suitable household food waste into a more manageable solid fuel product.

OBJECTIVES

Project Objectives

  • Develop an integrated drying, grinding and pelletizing concept for domestic use.
  • Reduce the moisture content of food waste before subsequent processing.
  • Reduce particle size to support downstream mixing and pelletizing.
  • Evaluate the prototype through experimental testing.

METHODOLOGY

Design & Methodology

  • Convective hot-air drying was used to reduce the moisture content.
  • A knife-cutting mechanism was used for size reduction.
  • Screw-based mixing and pelletizing concepts were considered for downstream processing.
  • Prototype performance was evaluated using repeated food-waste sample tests.

OUTCOMES

Results & Outcomes

  • A 400 g food-waste sample was tested.
  • Average moisture content decreased from 73.15% to 23.65% in 40 minutes across five replications.
  • Average particle size after crushing was approximately 0.78 mm.
  • The measured net calorific value of the produced pellets was approximately 12.99 MJ/kg.
